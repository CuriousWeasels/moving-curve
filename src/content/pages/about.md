I started Moving Curve as a way to hold myself accountable for my learning journey in finance and other matters of interest. Reading about valuation is one thing. Building the model, writing down a view and putting it where anyone can check it is another. Every piece here is a step I had to finish before I could publish it.

## Why “Moving Curve”

The name comes from the inchworm concept, an idea from performance coach Jared Tendler. It treats a skill not as a single level but as a bell curve. On any given day our work lands somewhere between our worst and our best, and most days fall in the middle.

Getting better means moving the whole curve forward. First the back edge catches up: our worst work stops being as bad as it used to be. Then the front edge reaches further than it ever has. Like an inchworm, the back moves up before the front can stretch out again.

This site is how I move my bell curve forward. Each new study pushes the front edge: a model or a method I hadn't built before. Going back to old work, checking what I got wrong and doing it better next time pulls the back edge along behind it.

## A note on the models

Models published here are simplified to make one idea clear. They are not investment advice, and they are not meant to be used to make investment decisions.
